# One-time financial plans, single-deliverable price anchors, and consumer willingness to pay for financial tools and AI guidance (US, as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Every figure is tagged with the year it describes or the date the source was checked. "(secondary)" marks a figure taken from a review site, aggregator or news reprint rather than the company or survey sponsor. "(search summary only)" marks a figure that came from a search-result summary whose underlying page I did not open, so treat it as lower-confidence.

## 1. What do one-time, flat-fee and advice-only planners charge, and what does a one-off retirement check-up or second opinion cost?

### Takeaway
A one-time plan from a human planner costs about $1,200–$3,500, with industry medians of roughly $1,700 (AdvicePay transaction data, 2025) to $3,000 (Kitces survey, 2024). A one-off retirement check-up or second opinion runs from $250 for a software-coaching session to $3,200 for a CFP-led checkup. App-delivered flat-fee planning firms charge $2,600–$9,000 a year. Every human one-time anchor is two to three orders of magnitude above a ~$5 calculator unlock.

### Cited Findings
**Industry medians and averages**
- Kitces Research (2024): the median fee for a standalone financial plan is **$3,000**, unchanged from 2022. Simpler plans average about $2,750 and the most extensive about $3,500 or more. Median hourly fee: $300. Typical subscription-planning annual fee: $4,500. (Secondary: these are SmartAsset's summary of the Kitces report; I could not open the Kitces report itself.) — [SmartAsset](https://smartasset.com/financial-advisor/financial-advisor-cost)
- An older Kitces figure of a **$2,400** median flat fee for a plan still circulates. It is undated and probably predates the $3,000 figure. — [SmartAsset advice-only page](https://www.smartasset.com/financial-advisor/advice-only-financial-advisor-services-and-fees); [Boomer & Echo](https://boomerandecho.com/weekend-reading-the-cost-of-a-financial-plan-edition/)
- AdvicePay 2026 Fee-for-Service Trend Report (2025 data from 525,000+ transactions; released Mar 18, 2026): the average **one-time fee is $1,676** (up 3.2% YoY). The average monthly subscription is $291 (up 4.7%) and the average quarterly subscription is $1,074 (up 9.4%). **85.6% of invoices were subscriptions.** The report presents subscriptions as the default model and describes one-time billing as harder to supervise. — [AdvicePay press release](https://blog.advicepay.com/press/advicepay-2026-fee-for-service-trend-report)
- AdvicePay 2024 report (2023 data): one-time payments rose 6.7% to **$1,578**. 83% of invoices were subscriptions and 74% were monthly recurring. — [News Direct / AdvicePay 2024 release](https://business.sherbrookerecord.com/sherbrookerecord/article/newsdirect-2024-4-3-advicepay-2024-fee-for-service-industry-trend-report-shows-marked-increase-in-fees-advisors-charge-for-fee-based-financial-planning)
- XY Planning Network: Michael Kitces cited a **$2,200** median annual planning fee across XYPN members. The article is undated and the figure is probably not current. I could not find XYPN's 2025 benchmarking median (the study was presented Oct 8, 2025, but the fee figures sit behind the full report). — [InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/ria-news/xy-planning-network-experiences-the-pains-of-rapid-growth/76241); [XYPN 2025 Benchmarking Highlights](https://www.xyplanningnetwork.com/412)

**Hourly and advice-only (Garrett Planning Network)**
- Most Garrett Planning Network advisers charge **$150–$300/hour**, or **$600–$1,500 for a one-time financial review**. The Kiplinger piece is undated and likely old. — [Kiplinger, "A One-Time Financial Checkup"](https://kiplinger.com/article/retirement/t023-c001-s002-a-one-time-financial-checkup.html)
- 2018: Sheryl Garrett said most hourly planners still charge about **$240/hour**. A typical engagement was two hours of consultation and review plus one hour to write the report. — [InvestmentNews (2018)](https://investmentnews.com/practice-management/hourly-pricing-model-has-benefits-and-pitfalls/74513)

**Retirement check-ups and second opinions**
- Boldin "My Plan Session": **$250 per session** for one-on-one coaching. It covers checking plan data, reviewing key metrics and agreeing next steps, and follow-ups can be booked (page checked Oct 2026). — [Boldin coaching](https://www.boldin.com/coaching/)
- Boldin Advisors "Retirement Plan Checkup": **$3,200**. It includes a 90-minute call with a CFP, stress tests, a portfolio review, a withdrawal strategy and a written Plan of Action. The free discovery session is $0. The page carries a Feb 8, 2022 publication date, so the price may have been updated since. — [Boldin Advisors engagement types](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/newretirement-advisors-engagement-types/)
- Third-party TrueAdvisor listings show Boldin Advisors fixed planning engagements up to $6,000 and hourly rates of $300–$475 (undated, secondary). — [TrueAdvisor](https://trueadvisor.com/firm/294881)
- Maximize My Social Security "Standard Plus": **$289/year**, which adds a one-hour expert phone review to the $49 software. This is effectively a ~$240 premium for a human review of a single report (checked Oct 2026). — [MMSS pricing](https://maximizemysocialsecurity.com/pricing)

**App-delivered flat-fee planning firms**

Strategic is $5,200/$4,200 and Comprehensive is $9,000/$8,000. Extra coaching is billed at $500/hour. NerdWallet calls Domain's entry price the highest among the flat-fee advisors it has reviewed. — [NerdWallet Domain Money review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/domain-money)

- Facet (May 2026 promotion terms): regular annual subscription of **$2,600 (Core), $4,300 (Plus) and $8,700 (Complete)**, plus a $300 enrollment fee waived for annual billing. (Search summary only for the tier prices: I did not open the legal document.) — [Facet May 2026 promotion terms](https://facet.com/legal-documents/may-promotion-1-2026/); NerdWallet (Oct 2025): "$2,600 and up per year" — [NerdWallet Facet review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/facet-wealth)
- Harness: standalone financial planning **starts at $1,200**, and its FAQ prices planning, tax and estate services at a **flat $1,000–$5,000 per service**. There is no minimum for a one-time plan. Harness refers clients to third-party firms and is paid for referrals. - Bank of Texas: **$1,500 one-time flat-fee plan**, generally for W-2-only clients (undated). — [Bank of Texas](https://www.bankoftexas.com/wealth/products-and-services/planning/financial-planning/flat-fee-financial-planning)

**Asset-based advice minimums (the "you need assets first" anchor)**
- Vanguard Personal Advisor has a **$50,000 minimum** and charges ~0.30%. Vanguard Digital Advisor has a $100 minimum (NerdWallet, 2025–2026, secondary). — [NerdWallet Vanguard PAS review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/vanguard-personal-advisor-services)
- Empower Personal Strategy has a **$100,000 minimum** and charges 0.49%–0.89%. Clients with $250k+ get two dedicated advisors (NerdWallet, secondary). — [NerdWallet Empower review](https://www.nerdwallet.com/financial-advisors/reviews/empower)
- Fidelity Wealth Services has a **$50,000 minimum**, and a dedicated advisor typically requires $500k+ (Fidelity's own page). — [Fidelity](https://fidelity.com/wealth-management/overview)

### Inferences
- The human-advice anchor ladder runs: ~$250 (software coaching session) → $600–$1,500 (hourly advice-only one-time review, dated) → ~$1,200–$1,700 (entry and average one-time engagements) → ~$3,000–$3,200 (median standalone plan / CFP retirement checkup) → $2,600–$9,000 per year (flat-fee planning subscriptions). A "retirement second opinion" from a human is a $250–$3,900 purchase.
- The supply side is moving away from one-time billing. AdvicePay subscriptions rose from 83% of invoices (2023) to 85.6% (2025), which mirrors the subscription bias Ask Linc already has. Advisors see one-time work as hard to scale and hard to supervise, not as unpopular with clients. That is AdvicePay's framing, not consumer evidence.
- The Domain Money Essential tier ($3,900) and MMSS Standard Plus (+$240 for one hour) suggest that much of what consumers pay for in a one-off is a human checking the plan, not the calculation itself.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Kitces 2024 fee report or the XYPN 2025 benchmarking report directly. NAPFA advice-only fee data, Advice-Only Financial (the firm) pricing and Garrett Planning's current hourly rates were not found in a dated 2024–2026 primary source.
- I could not verify Facet's tier prices on a Facet page I opened myself.

## 2. What do consumers already pay for a single personal-finance deliverable in adjacent categories?

### Takeaway
Single, non-advice deliverables cluster at **$15–$50** (a state tax return at $15.99, a Carfax report at ~$44.99, a Social Security optimizer at $49 a year, a 30-day AI retirement sprint at $49). DIY planning software clusters at **$99–$199 a year**. Several high-quality alternatives are free, which caps what a calculator output alone can command.

### Cited Findings
**Social Security**
- Maximize My Social Security: Standard household plan **$49/year** (unlimited what-ifs) and Standard Plus **$289/year** (adds a 1-hour expert review). Subscriptions auto-renew (checked Oct 2026). — [MMSS pricing](https://maximizemysocialsecurity.com/pricing)
- Historical: $40/year household license plus $150 for an optional expert review (date not stated, likely around 2016–2018). — [Money.com](https://money.com/collection-post/social-security-calculators-lifetime-benefits/)
- Open Social Security is free according to the research brief. I did not independently verify this in this pass.

**Tax software (2026 filing season, tax year 2025)**
- FreeTaxUSA: federal return free; **state return $15.99** (Toolradar checked Sep 25, 2026; SuperMoney verified Mar 27, 2026). Other sources say $14.99. Add-on support is $7.99 (Deluxe) and $44.99 (Pro), though another source lists Pro at $64.99. — [Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/freetaxusa/pricing); [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/tax-preparation/freetaxusa)
- TurboTax Online: Deluxe federal is roughly **$69–$79**, Premium federal roughly **$99–$139**, plus about **$39–$64 per state**. Secondary sources conflict and I did not open Intuit's own page. A Frontdeskreview listing cites $139 for Deluxe at full price, an outlier. — [Frontdeskreview (Jun 2026)](https://frontdeskreview.com/software/tax-software/turbotax/); [Wealthvieu](https://wealthvieu.com/taxes/tax-filing/turbotax-review/)

**Credit reports and scores**
- myFICO paid tiers are monthly subscriptions of about **$19.95–$39.95/month**, with a free tier (SuperMoney verified Dec 30, 2025, secondary). I found no current single-report price. — [SuperMoney myFICO review](https://www.supermoney.com/reviews/credit-reporting/myfico); [MoneyLion myFICO review (Apr 2026)](https://www.moneylion.com/learn/credit/credit-report/myfico-review)

**Vehicle history (Carfax-style one-off report)**
- Carfax single report: **~$44.99**, per several third-party sources. Bundles are around $64.99–$74.99 for three reports, and the figures conflict. These are low-quality secondary sources, and I did not verify the price on carfax.com. — [AmeriFreight](https://www.amerifreight.net/information/carfax-review-2024-trusted-or-flawed); [Carwhere](https://www.carwhere.com/carfax-alternative)

**DIY retirement, planning and budgeting software (annual anchors)**
- ProjectionLab: Basic **free**, Premium **$129/year**, Pro (advisor) $549/year. Only annual billing is shown, with no lifetime option on the official page (checked Oct 2026). A third-party review claims a lifetime option, which conflicts with the official page. — [ProjectionLab pricing](https://projectionlab.com/pricing); contradicted by [Gerald review](https://joingerald.com/learn/saving--investing/projectionlab-review-features-pricing-alternatives)
- Boldin PlannerPlus: **$144/year** for new subscribers, rising to **$168/year on Oct 15, 2026**. Existing subscribers keep their price. — [Boldin announcement](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/plannerplus-price-increase-october-2026/)
- YNAB: **$109/year** or $14.99/month, with a 34-day free trial. Monarch Core: **$99.99/year** or $14.99/month. Monarch Plus: $199/year (2026, secondary). — [Costbench YNAB vs Monarch](https://costbench.com/compare/ynab-vs-monarch-money/); [Finny comparison](https://getfinny.app/blog/ynab-vs-monarch-money-2026)
- The Complete Retirement Planner (Excel) sells for a **one-time $89.99** (2026 version). (Search summary only.) — [Quicken blog](https://www.quicken.com/blog/cost-effective-personal-finance-apps-for-retirement-planning/)

### Inferences
- Consumers routinely pay **$15–$50 for a single, clearly scoped financial artifact** that serves a transaction they are about to make (filing taxes, buying a car, claiming Social Security). Those purchases are tied to a deadline or a transaction. A retirement or Coast FIRE answer has no such trigger, which probably weakens willingness to pay compared with a Carfax report or a state return.
- **~$5 sits well below every paid single-deliverable anchor found.** The cheapest is a $15.99 state return. That makes $5 an impulse price rather than a value-anchored one. It also sits close to free, where strong alternatives exist (ProjectionLab Basic, Open Social Security, many free calculators).
- MMSS ($49 a year for unlimited what-ifs) is the closest structural analog to "pay to unlock a calculator for a period". Its price is about 10x a $5-per-week offer and it is billed annually.

### Gaps
- No dated pricing found for paid Medicare or benefits analyses (for example, Medicare plan-comparison reports) or for paid home-affordability reports. RentCast or appraisal-style one-off reports were not researched.
- The free annual credit report entitlement and the current myFICO one-time 3-bureau report price were not verified in this pass.

## 3. Survey and market data on willingness to pay for budgeting and planning apps, and for AI financial advice; trust in AI for money decisions (2023–2026)

### Takeaway
The evidence on paying for AI financial guidance specifically is negative to lukewarm. In 2023, 80% of Americans said they would not pay for AI financial insights, including retirement insights. Trust in AI to create a retirement plan trails trust in humans by about 4:1 (2025). In 2026, however, a majority of AI users report paying for some AI tool, so the barrier appears to be finance-specific trust rather than paying for AI at all. Human verification raises comfort substantially.

### Cited Findings
**Willingness to pay for AI financial advice**
- Ipsos/Yahoo Finance (June 2–4, 2023; n=1,276, KnowledgePanel): **80% would not pay** for AI insights or recommendations on market trends, investments, **retirement**, personal finances or other financial matters. 63% do not trust AI to advise them on their finances. 57% see no area where an AI advisor beats a human; only 13% see an advantage for retirement insights. Among investors, 20% are likely and 66% unlikely to use an AI financial advisor. Top concerns: no human element (46%), not knowing enough about it (45%), technology too new (44%), inaccurate information (39%). — [Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-us/one-five-investors-report-using-ai-chatbot-past-year)
- Howdy (consumer survey of 1,000+ Americans, coverage July 2026): **54% would drop an AI tool if they had to pay** for it, 26% currently pay for an AI platform and 18% say AI makes them money. Secondary: press coverage only, and the base of the 54% is ambiguous. — [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/?p=1952576); [CMM Online](https://cmmonline.com/news/over-half-would-stop-using-ai-if-it-required-payment)
- Menlo Ventures / Morning Consult (July 2026; n=5,067, nationally representative): **55% of AI users pay for at least one AI product**, and the typical payer spends $20–$49/month. Plans: 50% flat monthly, 26% annual, 17% usage-based, 15% subscription plus usage. Only 23% of people who pay bills use AI to help. The report says almost nothing in consumer AI is priced or designed around the household. It cites roughly 3% of AI users paying in 2025, a jump that likely reflects a methodology change; I treat the year-over-year comparison with caution. — [Menlo Ventures](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/)
- This conflicts with payment data: an a16z analysis of PNC data found only **2.2% of consumers** paying for AI, at about $31/month (search summary only; primary not opened). Bloomberg (Oct 6, 2026) reports that "few consumers pay". The gap is likely method (bank-transaction detection versus self-report). — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-10-06/ai-subscription-models-tested-as-few-consumers-pay)
- Kitces (Oct 2025) on Origin's $1-then-$99/year "AI financial advisor": Origin would need to raise fees "3X, 5X, or 10X" to break even per client, and it "remains to be seen" whether users will pay anything for AI advice. This is expert opinion. — [Kitces AdvisorTech Oct 2025](https://www.kitces.com/blog/the-latest-in-financial-advisortech-october-2025-origin-ai-financial-advisor-low-fee-stockopter-grantd/)
- Janus Henderson (2026 investor survey): clients say they would pay similar fees whether or not their human advisor uses AI, with no evidence of fee compression. This concerns AI-assisted humans, not AI-only advice. — [Janus Henderson](https://www.janushenderson.com/en-us/advisor/article/investor-survey-does-ai-usage-impact-advisory-fee-sensitivity/)

**Trust in AI for money decisions**
- Northwestern Mutual Planning & Progress 2025 (Harris Poll, Jan 2–19, 2025; n=4,626): for **creating a retirement plan, 56% prefer a human advisor versus 13% AI**. For asking a financial question it is 55% versus 15%. 47% would prefer an advisor who uses AI (54% of Gen Z and Millennials versus 36% of Boomers+). The sponsor sells human advice. — [Northwestern Mutual release (Aug 5, 2025)](https://news.northwesternmutual.com/2025-08-05-Human-Connection-Over-Machines-Americans-Trust-Advisors-More-Than-AI-for-Financial-Advice,-Finds-Northwestern-Mutuals-2025-Planning-Progress-Study)
- NIRS / Greenwald (fieldwork Oct 24–Nov 14, 2025; n=1,203, age 25+; released Aug 26, 2026): 63% have used an AI tool, but **61% have not used AI for questions about their finances, investments or retirement**. 45% are uncomfortable with AI playing a role in financial advice. Interest in AI help: budgeting 38%, investing 34%, **retirement planning 32%**, paying down debt 30%, tax planning 24%. — [NIRS via PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/80-of-americans-say-us-faces-retirement-crisis-amid-affordability-pressures-and-debt-americans-wary-of-ai-financial-advice-and-crypto-in-retirement-plans-302860171.html)
- FINRA Investor Education Foundation (fielded Feb 2024; 1,000+ adults): only **5% used AI** as a source of financial information, against 63% who consulted financial professionals and 56% friends and family; the figure was 10% among 18–29-year-olds. In the trust experiment, trust in AI-attributed versus professional-attributed content was similar for stock and bond projections (34% versus 33%) but lower for portfolio allocation (30% versus 37%). — [FINRA news release (2024)](https://www.finra.org/media-center/newsreleases/2024/report-finra-foundation-financial-professional-or-artificial-intelligence)
- CFP Board (July 2023): 51% of investors have little or no trust in generative-AI advice, and 31% would act on it without checking another source. **52% would be comfortable acting on genAI planning advice once a CFP professional has verified it.** — [CFP Board 2023](https://www.cfp.net/news/2023/08/cfp-board-survey-investors-trust-ai-more-than-social-media--but-advice-still)
- CFP Board (April 2025; n=1,000+ aged 25–64 with household income above $50k): 44% of younger versus 26% of older respondents trust AI for financial advice. 57% have made a regrettable decision based on misleading online information. "Outdated AI-generated advice" ranks among the most frequently encountered problems. — [CFP Board 2025 release](https://www.cfp.net/news/2025/06/bad-online-advice-leads-majority-of-americans-to-make-regrettable-financial-decisions); [report PDF](https://www.cfp.net/-/media/files/cfp-board/knowledge/reports-and-research/consumer-surveys/cfp-board-financial-misinformation-report-2025.pdf)
- TD Bank / Ipsos (late March 2025; n=2,500): 22% see no benefit to AI in financial services and 50% say AI will not give fair, unbiased financial advice. Only 18% are comfortable letting AI make important financial decisions on its own (the 18% figure is search summary only). — [TD Bank](https://stories.td.com/us/en/article/td-bank-survey-finds-americans-are-ready-to-embrace-ai-but-have-yet-to-unlock-its-full-potential)

**Budgeting and fintech apps**
- Plaid Fintech Effect (2022): 80% of US consumers use digital financial apps and 48% use fintech to manage finances daily. I found **no Plaid statistic on paying for apps**. — [Plaid 2022](https://plaid.com/blog/fintech-effect-report-2022/)
- Budgeting-app usage is low. Debt.com (2021): 9% use budgeting apps. A Walnut 2026 compilation (from Debt.com 2026) puts it at 22%. PYMNTS (2025): "daily budget app adoption stalls at 14%" (headline only). These figures conflict and their methods differ. — [Debt.com](https://www.debt.com/press-release/americans-are-budgeting-now-more-than-ever-and-its-getting-them-out-of-debt-says-debt-com-survey/); [Walnut](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/budgeting-statistics); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/mobile/2025/daily-budget-app-adoption-stalls-at-14-percent/)

### Inferences
- The gap between "most AI users now pay for some AI" (Menlo 2026) and "80% would not pay for AI financial insights" (Ipsos 2023) suggests the obstacle is **trust in AI for money**, not reluctance to pay for AI. The Ipsos figure is three years old, though, and no 2025–2026 survey asked the same question.
- Retirement planning is the task where trust in AI is weakest (13% versus 56% favouring humans, NM 2025). A paid retirement answer from AI is therefore likely to face more resistance than a paid budgeting or cash-flow answer. Budgeting is the top AI-interest use case in NIRS (38% versus 32% for retirement).
- The CFP Board finding (52% comfortable once a CFP has verified the advice) and MMSS's paid human review both suggest that **"verified" or "human-reviewed" is the attribute consumers pay a premium for**. That is a positioning lever, not evidence that a $5 automated answer will sell.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 US survey found that tests specific price points for AI-generated financial answers or reports.
- No J.D. Power, Cerulli or Morning Consult (finance-specific) willingness-to-pay figures for AI advice were found in this pass. Bankrate and NerdWallet surveys on paying for finance apps were not found.

## 4. Subscription fatigue versus one-time purchases in fintech; how often people revisit retirement calculations

### Takeaway
General subscription fatigue is well documented in 2025–2026 consumer surveys, but I found **no fintech-specific evidence that consumers prefer one-time purchases**. Retirement planning is episodic: most people do not review their plan annually, and historically only about 40–46% have ever tried to calculate their retirement needs. That supports an occasional-purchase pattern, with the caveat that most of this data is old.

### Cited Findings
**Subscription fatigue (general consumer)**
- MarketWatch Guides survey (2025, via syndicated local TV, Oct 7, 2025): **one in three Americans are canceling subscriptions to save money**, and **60% cancel one subscription before starting another**. Free trials are a common leak. (Secondary.) — [KWTX / Gray TV](https://www.kwtx.com/2025/10/07/subscription-fatigue-leading-some-unsubscribe/)
- NerdWallet survey (2026, via syndicated local TV, Apr 7, 2026): **55% want to significantly reduce subscription spending in 2026**. (Secondary.) — [KPTV](https://www.kptv.com/2026/04/07/more-than-half-americans-plan-cut-subscriptions-2026-survey-finds/)
- Supply side: AdvicePay's 2025 data shows advice billing moving toward subscriptions (85.6% of invoices), not away from them. — [AdvicePay](https://blog.advicepay.com/press/advicepay-2026-fee-for-service-trend-report)
- Boldin is raising PlannerPlus prices for new subscribers only ($144 to $168 a year, Oct 15, 2026), so existing subscribers keep their rate. That suggests the company prioritises retention. — [Boldin](https://www.boldin.com/retirement/plannerplus-price-increase-october-2026/)

**How often people revisit retirement plans**
- Voya (2016): **80% had not reviewed or revised their retirement plan in the past year**, and 53% did not review it even after a financial life change. (Secondary, old.) — [BenefitsPRO (2016)](https://benefitspro.com/2016/12/13/waiting-in-line-at-the-dmv-less-painful-than-retir)
- TD Ameritrade (reported Jan 2020): about half of workers had adjusted their retirement approach at some point, and of those, 53% had done so at least three times. — [Motley Fool (2020)](https://www.fool.com/retirement/2020/01/26/reassuring-news-those-behind-retirement-savings.aspx)
- EBRI Retirement Confidence Survey: the share of workers who have tried to calculate their retirement savings needs held **around 40%** for a decade (about 2011). It was 46% in 2013, when 45% said they guess. (Old data.) — [EBRI "Guess Work?"](https://www.ebri.org/content/guess-work); [PlanSponsor](https://www.plansponsor.com/saving-for-retirement-easier-said-than-done/)
- FRTIB TSP Financial Wellness Survey (2025): **55% in 2025 versus 47% in 2023** said they have an idea how much they need to retire comfortably. This covers federal plan participants only. — [FRTIB (Oct 2025)](https://www.frtib.gov/pdf/minutes/2025/Oct/att5-FRTIB-Financial-Wellness-Survey-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- Episodic engagement (most people do not revisit annually) fits a pay-per-answer or time-boxed pass better than an auto-renewing subscription for calculator users. "60% cancel one before starting another" suggests a new recurring charge has to displace an existing one, while a one-off charge does not.
- Low engagement also means low demand frequency. Even willing payers would buy rarely, so revenue per lead from one-time calculator charges would be small unless it converts to the subscription.

### Gaps
- No recent (2023–2026) fintech-specific survey found comparing preference for one-time purchases against subscriptions.
- No recent national data found on how often individuals rerun retirement calculators. Most review-frequency data is from 2016–2020 and EBRI's calculation data from 2011–2013.

## 5. Who uses FIRE and Coast FIRE calculators, and are they price-sensitive?

### Takeaway
I found **no demographic data specific to Coast FIRE calculator users**. The best proxies (r/financialindependence and White Coat Investor surveys) describe a self-selected, high-income, financially literate, male-skewed, DIY audience. That means high ability to pay, but a culture of DIY and frugality, and a reliance on free tools.

### Cited Findings
- r/financialindependence 2023 survey (results posted May 5, 2024) is a spreadsheet of responses. One commenter computed average non-investment wages of **$285,619** and an average primary residence value of $575,032. These are means from a self-selected sample and are anecdotal. The 2025 survey data was released around May 2026. — [Reddit mirror, 2023 results thread](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/financialindependence/comments/1cl177n/the_official_2023_survey_results_are_here/); [2025 survey mention](https://rl.klein.ruhr/r/FinancialIndependence)
- In an earlier r/FI survey (2018), a single outlier with $80.6M in income moved the average by about $43k, so medians should be used. — [Reddit mirror (2018)](https://reddit.rtrace.io/r/financialindependence/comments/8ai335/initial_financial_independence_survey_results_are/)
- White Coat Investor 2024 survey (n=1,897, physician-focused FI audience): respondents skew wealthier, more financially literate, with less debt, and mostly male. An undated WCI post says a typical household makes $100k–$1M (mostly $250k–$500k) and over half are millionaires. — [WCI 2024 results](https://www.whitecoatinvestor.com/2024-wci-annual-survey-results/)
- Credit Karma (May 2023): 53% of Gen Z identify with FIRE, yet 32% of those have no retirement savings. This is secondary and covers only Gen Z. — [Credit Karma](https://www.creditkarma.com/author/stephaniecookck/feed)
- Kitces (Oct 2025, opinion): AI-advice consumers such as Origin's are "DIY users, presumably younger and less affluent than the typical RIA client", focused on cash flow and homeownership and unlikely to hire human planners. — [Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/the-latest-in-financial-advisortech-october-2025-origin-ai-financial-advisor-low-fee-stockopter-grantd/)
- A ChooseFI audience survey or media kit was not found.

### Inferences
- FIRE and Coast FIRE users likely have the means to pay $5–$50. But they skew toward DIY and spreadsheets and toward free tools (ProjectionLab Basic, community calculators), so a **paywall on a calculator result risks reading as against the community's norms**. This is opinion and inference, not measured.
- Coast FIRE in particular appeals to younger savers (the concept is about stopping contributions early). The Gen Z FIRE-identification data and Kitces's description of AI-advice users suggest younger, lower-asset users. That group has the least ability to pay and the highest AI comfort (CFP Board 2025: 44% versus 26%).

### Gaps
- No Coast FIRE calculator user demographics, no ChooseFI audience data, and no FIRE-community survey on paying for tools were found. The r/FI medians would require downloading the spreadsheet (not done).

## 6. AI-native financial planning startups charging one-time fees (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
I found **almost no AI-native consumer product charging a true one-time fee for a plan or report**. The market is dominated by free AI coaches and low-priced annual subscriptions (~$99–$149 a year). The closest one-time-style offer is a **$49 30-day pass**.

### Cited Findings
- My Plan Keeper (AI retirement assistant "Grace"): **$49 "30-Day Clarity Sprint"** gives 30 days of AI access with the personalised plan saved permanently, and the **$149/year** "Retirement Co-Pilot" is founding-member pricing. The page's date could not be confirmed. — [My Plan Keeper](https://myplankeeper.com/)
- Origin: "AI financial advisor" at **$1 for the first year, then $99/year**, bundling budgeting, investing and planning projections (Oct 2025). — [Kitces](https://www.kitces.com/blog/the-latest-in-financial-advisortech-october-2025-origin-ai-financial-advisor-low-fee-stockopter-grantd/)
- RetirePlanAI: free tier, with paid **$9.99/month** for unlimited AI coach access and detailed reports (date unclear). — [RetirePlanAI pricing](https://www.retireplanai.com/pricing/)
- Frugal: a **free** direct-to-consumer AI financial coach that enrolled just under 2 million users in its first quarter (CFP Board, Oct 2025; search summary only). — [CFP Board AI report (Oct 2025)](https://www.cfp.net/-/media/files/cfp-board/knowledge/reports-and-research/harnessing-ai-in-the-financial-planning-profession-cfp-board-report.pdf)
- Forbes (Nov 2025): newer entrants Hiro and Tendi are eliminating advisory fees and focusing on conversational AI (secondary). — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2025/11/06/ai-powered-financial-planning-and-the-rise-of-personalized-financial-independence-tools/)
- Free AI retirement calculators exist, for example MoneyCoach.ai. — [MoneyCoach](https://moneycoach.ai/free-tools/retirement-calculator)

### Inferences
- In AI-native consumer planning, the price trend is toward free or near-free entry ($0, $1 for the first year, $9.99 a month), with revenue from annual subscriptions, AUM or referrals. A paid one-time AI answer would be swimming against what competitors give away. A $49 30-day pass is the only analog found to the "~$5 for a week" idea, and it is about 2.5x the weekly rate.
- Taken with section 3 (trust penalty on retirement plans, 80% unwilling to pay for AI financial insights in 2023), the evidence suggests that a calculator result alone has weak standalone paid value. What commands money in adjacent markets is (a) a transaction-tied report, (b) a human review or verification, or (c) ongoing tooling at ~$100–$170 a year. This is inference.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024–2026 launches of AI-native consumer products selling a single AI-generated financial plan or report at a fixed one-time price, and no data on pay-what-you-want or credit-based pricing in consumer fintech. I cannot say whether such products do not exist or were simply not surfaced by search.
- Conversion or uptake data for any paid one-off calculator unlock was not found.
