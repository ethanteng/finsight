# Consumer budgeting, cash-flow forecasting and AI money-assistant pricing (as of October 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-07. Prices marked **[official, seen 2026-10-07]** come from the vendor's own page or App Store listing, fetched that day. Everything else is from third-party reviews, trackers or press, with dates where known. Several aggregators (CostBench, Subger, SoftwareSuggest) were often stale or garbled, so they are cited only where nothing better exists and are flagged.

---

## 1. What do competitors charge, and how? (master comparison)

### Takeaway
The category settles on roughly **$8–$9/month on annual plans ($95–$109/yr) and $13–$15/month month-to-month**, sold as subscriptions with 7–34-day trials. The exceptions are free, data- or advice-monetised products (Empower, Credit Karma), pay-what-you-want or choose-your-price models (Lunch Money, Rocket Money), lifetime licences (PocketGuard, Moneydance), tiers that gate by forecast horizon (PocketSmith), and expensive wealth or planning tiers ($129–$2,499+/yr). In 2026 the market moved toward a second, higher tier ($15/mo to $299/yr) built on forecasting or an AI agent. Meanwhile ChatGPT made bank-linked AI finance Q&A free to US users.

### Inferences
- **Price anchors Ask Linc will be compared to:** ~$95–$109/yr (Copilot, Monarch Core, Tiller, YNAB, Origin) and ~$13–$15/mo (Copilot, Monarch, YNAB, Rocket Money Premium+). Cheaper anchors: Simplifi $3.99–$6.99/mo annual, Goodbudget $80/yr, Lunch Money from $60/yr. A one-time ~$5 is below every major monthly price, so it reads as a low-commitment trial price rather than a discount on the product.
- **2026 trend:** incumbents are adding a **second, higher tier built around forecasting or an AI agent** (Monarch Plus $299/yr; Rocket Money Premium+ $15/mo with Rowan; Cleo Pro AI coaching). Forecasting and agentic AI are being used to justify an upsell rather than given away. Over the same period, **general-purpose AI (ChatGPT Finances, now reaching Free users; Perplexity) is giving away linked-account Q&A**, which squeezes any price for AI Q&A alone.
- Seen from the AI side, bundling is the norm. No major consumer budgeting app sells AI Q&A as a separate usage-metered add-on. Metering appears only as vague "limited vs unlimited" tiers (PortfolioPilot) or capability tiers (Cleo, Albert).

## 2. Who charges for cash-flow forecasting specifically, and in which tier?

### Takeaway
There is **no single norm**. Short-horizon cash-flow projection (upcoming bills, projected balances) is often **in the base or only paid plan** (Simplifi, PocketSmith, Empower free). **Long-horizon, scenario or "life" forecasting is increasingly a premium tier** (Monarch Plus $299/yr, ProjectionLab Premium $129/yr, PocketSmith charging more for longer horizons). Standalone daily-balance forecasters price at **$4.99–$7.99/mo or $50–$70/yr**.

### Cited Findings
- **Quicken Simplifi** includes "projected cash flow" (future balances from income, bills, spending; "what-if" scenarios) in its single plan at $3.99–$6.99/mo billed annually — [quicken.com/simplifi](https://www.quicken.com/simplifi).
- **PocketSmith** makes forecast horizon the paid differentiator: Free 6 months, Foundation 10 years ($9.99/mo annual), Flourish 30 years ($16.66/mo annual), Fortune 60 years ($26.66/mo annual). "Calendar" and "Cashflow" are in "all plans" — [PocketSmith plans](https://my.pocketsmith.com/plans).
- **Monarch** put "Forecasting" in its new **Plus** tier ($299/yr new members; $199 first year for existing members), launched April 2026, separate from Core ($99.99/yr) — [PR Newswire/Yahoo, Apr 21, 2026](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/monarch-launches-premium-tier-monarch-124800073.html). A competitor describes Monarch Core's Cash Flow as "a budgeting output, not a checking-balance projection," and Plus forecasting as multi-year, desktop-web only — [Centinel, Apr 2026](https://www.centinelmoney.com/resources/centinel-vs-monarch-money). Doughsense calls it "a projection surface sold on its Plus tier" — [Doughsense](https://doughsense.com/compare/monarch).
- **ProjectionLab** gives forecasting and Monte Carlo away free, but "Cash-Flow Projections" and "What-If Scenarios" are **Premium ($129/yr)** features — [ProjectionLab pricing](https://projectionlab.com/pricing).
- **Copilot** ($95/yr) does not advertise forecasting; its cash flow view is historical trends — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/copilot-track-budget-money/id1447330651).
- **YNAB** and **Tiller** pricing pages advertise no forecasting — [YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/pricing), [Tiller](https://tiller.com/pricing/).
- **Empower**'s free dashboard includes a cash flow tracker — [bestroboadvisors](https://www.bestroboadvisors.org/empower-review/).
- Standalone forecasters: Centinel Premium $7.99/mo or $69.99/yr (2-month daily checking forecast) — [Centinel](https://www.centinelmoney.com/resources/centinel-vs-monarch-money); Kualto $4.99/mo to $49.99/yr — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1217021017); Doughsense Plus £10/mo or £80/yr — [Doughsense](https://doughsense.com/compare/monarch); Budget Calendar $2.99 one-time; Paymnt $0.99 one-time — [Budget Calendar](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/budget-calendar/id951672394), [apppricinglab](https://apppricinglab.com/app/apple/6753765226).

### Inferences
- Ask Linc's forecast (recurring streams, daily rate, planned events, card payoff projections) is closest to **Simplifi's projected cash flow plus PocketSmith's calendar forecast plus Centinel's daily shortfall alert**, which the market prices at roughly **$4–$10/mo**, not at Monarch Plus levels. Monarch Plus's $299 buys life-planning, business and estate features as well as forecasting.
- Card payoff projection (balance and interest under payoff plans) was not seen advertised as a distinct paid feature by any competitor reviewed. It may be a differentiator, but this is unverified (absence of evidence).
- PocketSmith's horizon gating is a precedent for monetising forecasting by **depth or horizon** rather than by time-boxed access.

### Gaps
- Did not verify whether Rocket Money, Copilot or Monarch Core show "upcoming bills / projected balance" widgets that function as light forecasting (their marketing pages don't call it forecasting).
- No usage or conversion data from any vendor on how many users pay specifically for forecasting.

## 3. One-time passes, pay-per-report, credits / usage-based AI pricing: has anyone tried it, and what happened?

### Takeaway
Among major US consumer finance apps, **no one sells a non-renewing time-boxed pass (for example $5 for a week) or AI credits**. The nearest precedents are: **paid trials** (Kubera $1 for 14 days, no auto-renew; Origin's reported $1 first year), **lifetime licences** (PocketGuard $149.99, Moneydance, small iOS apps), **weekly auto-renewing subscriptions** in low-end iOS AI budget apps ($4.99/week), **pay-per-outcome or per-session fees** (Rocket Money's 35–60% bill-negotiation fee; Origin's $99/$149 human sessions), and **vague AI "limited vs unlimited" tiers** (PortfolioPilot). No outcome data was found for any of them.

### Cited Findings
- **Kubera**: the trial "requires a nominal fee of $1, which is non-refundable," and "your subscription will not automatically begin after the trial" — [Kubera help](https://help.kubera.com/article/117-why-pay-1-for-the-trial). Third parties describe it as 14 days — [WallStreetZen via search](https://www.wallstreetzen.com/blog/?p=36381).
- **Origin**: 2026 promo "first full year for just $1 — then it renews at $99/yr"; one review says it requires a card at signup and that the free trial is suspended while the promo runs — [fincomparelab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/?p=959) (third-party). Human planner sessions cost **$99 (30 min) / $149 (60 min)** each — same source.
- **Rocket Money** bill negotiation: "a one-time fee of 35–60% of your first year's savings," charged only if savings are achieved — [Rocket Money Help Center](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2217739-how-much-does-rocket-money-cost). Premium+ ($15/mo) removes the success fee — [Help Center](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/16802386-introduction-to-premium).
- **PortfolioPilot** meters AI by tier: Gold ($20–29/mo) "AI assistant (limited access)," Platinum ($49–99/mo) "AI assistant (unlimited)"; no published quota — [PortfolioPilot pricing](https://portfoliopilot.com/pricing).
- **FinanceBrain**: three free questions, then a ~$20/mo subscription for unlimited (third-party listing; low confidence) — [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/financebrain/discuss).
- **Cleo** meters AI by capability (Pro's "advanced AI coaching, voice chat, expanded memory" at $8.99/mo), not by message count — [toolsforhumans](https://www.toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/cleo); [fincomparelab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/?p=909).
- **Lifetime/one-time**: PocketGuard $149.99 lifetime (web only, Sept 2026) — [getfinny](https://getfinny.app/blog/pocketguard-pricing-2026); Moneydance perpetual licence plus optional ~$40/yr bank sync, trial capped at 100 transactions rather than time — [Moneydance KB](https://infinitekind.tenderapp.com/kb/purchasing-and-upgrading/purchase-options); Paymnt $0.99 one-time; Budget Calendar $2.99 — see table 1B. A competitor claims "YNAB, Monarch Money, Copilot, Quicken Simplifi, EveryDollar — are subscription-only with no one-time option at any price" — [Pocket Clear blog](https://pocketclear.app/blog/cheapest-budgeting-apps-2026.html) (vendor claim).
- **Weekly subscriptions**: a small iOS "Budget Planner & Tracker" offers 1 week $4.99, 1 month $12.99, 1 year $39.99, lifetime $59.99 — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6748604085). Annualised, $4.99/week ≈ $259/yr (computed).
- **Multi-term short subscriptions**: Kualto sells 3-month ($14.99) and 6-month ($29.99) terms alongside monthly and annual — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1217021017).
- **Choose-your-price** is established at scale: Rocket Money Premium "pay what you think is fair," typically $7–$14/mo — [Rocket Money](https://www.rocketmoney.com/learn/personal-finance/how-much-does-rocket-money-cost); Lunch Money PWYW annual (see Q4).
- **ChatGPT Finances** has no per-feature fee and is reaching the Free tier, so linked-account AI Q&A at zero marginal price is now available from the largest AI brand — [Plaid blog](https://plaid.com/blog/chatgpt-personal-finance-plaid/); [Releasebot](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai/chatgpt).

### Inferences
- A **$5 one-week pass for a forecast** has no direct precedent among recognisable competitors. The closest is Kubera's $1 paid trial that does not auto-renew, which is framed as a seriousness filter rather than a revenue line.
- Weekly pricing ($4.99/week) is associated with low-reputation iOS paywall apps. A "week pass" may carry that connotation unless it is clearly non-renewing.
- Per-use or outcome pricing exists where the value event is discrete and measurable (bill negotiation savings, a human advisor session). A one-off forecast report fits that pattern better than "AI questions" do, since AI Q&A is being commoditised by ChatGPT and Perplexity.
- Credits/points budgets for AI Q&A in consumer finance were **not observed**. Nearest is PortfolioPilot's undefined "limited access." Introducing visible credits would be novel and would need explaining in the UI.

### Gaps
- **No outcome data** (conversion, revenue, churn) was found for any paid trial, lifetime plan, weekly plan or per-session model in consumer finance.
- Did not research TurboTax/H&R Block per-return pricing or credit-bureau one-off report pricing (classic pay-per-report precedents in consumer finance).
- Did not research Plaid per-Item or per-call costs, which determine whether a one-time $5 pass with live bank linking is margin-positive.

## 4. Lunch Money's pay-what-you-want: bands, what customers chose, and whether they moved away

### Takeaway
Lunch Money (bootstrapped, founder Jen Yip) **still uses PWYW for annual plans as of 2026**, alongside a fixed $10/mo plan. It has raised the floor twice ($40 → $50 → $60) with lifetime price locks for existing subscribers. The founder's July 2024 post reports that ARPU fell, but MRR nearly doubled in about a year, churn and trial conversion improved, only 10% of legacy users downgraded, and the ~37% paying over $100/yr generate over 60% of revenue. The Mint shutdown confounds the attribution.

### Cited Findings
- **Price history:** launched August 2019 at $3/mo or $29/yr; monthly raised to $8 by March 2020; January 2021 raised to **$10/mo or $100/yr** (with a 50% first-year discount) — [Lunch Money blog, "Why we lowered our pricing…," Jul 16, 2024](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/we-lowered-our-pricing).
- **Switch to PWYW:** "In February 2023, [we] changed our annual pricing plan to a pay-what-you-want model" starting at $40. Rationale: "Our previous pricing model at $100 per year shuts people out who could really benefit," given "regional economic variability, big life changes, hard times" — [Lunch Money blog, "The New Era," May 21, 2024](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/the-new-era).
- **Bands:** "a pay-what-you-want pricing model for annual plans, starting from $40 up to $150 per year"; the monthly plan stayed at $10 — [Lunch Money, Jul 2024](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/we-lowered-our-pricing).
- **Stated reasons:** retention and lower churn, differentiation as a bootstrapped alternative to VC-funded apps, reach and community, a simpler Stripe catalogue with no coupons, and the ability to experiment without revenue pressure. The founder also cites a personal values shift and a podcaster's sliding-scale idea — same source.
- **Outcomes (founder-reported, Jul 2024):**
  - "Only 10% of legacy users switched to a lower-priced plan."
  - "About 37% of users on the new plan pay above $100 annually (including monthly $10 subscribers), and they generate over 60% of revenue."
  - ARPU decreased; active subscribers rose sharply in the prior ~10 months, "mostly from word-of-mouth after Mint shut down"; **MRR "nearly doubled"** over roughly a year.
  - Churn and trial conversion are "healthier than ever" (no percentages), partly helped by a new pause-instead-of-cancel option.
  - Caveats noted by the founder: Mint's shutdown muddies attribution; an August 2023 spam-signup surge skewed rates; some ex-Mint users may have chosen $40/yr against competitors' $90–$120/yr.
  - No average chosen price or full distribution was published — [Lunch Money, Jul 2024](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/we-lowered-our-pricing).
- **Floor raise 1:** minimum $40 → **$50/yr**, effective Oct 22, 2024; "your price is locked in at the rate you came in at, or whatever you last selected"; to fund "new features and improvements, community initiatives and team expansions." No PWYW data given — [Lunch Money, "The Least Annoying Price Increase in History," Oct 16, 2024](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/the-least-annoying-price-increase-in-history).
- **Floor raise 2:** minimum $50 → **$60/yr** effective **Mar 15, 2026**; existing subscribers keep their rate while active, and sign-ups before Mar 15 could lock $50 for life; justified by product improvements, reliability, sustainability and independence — [Lunch Money, Jan 12, 2026](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/an-update-to-our-annual-plan-minimum-effective-march-15-2026).
- **Earlier scale markers:** $100k ARR announced May 2021; ~200 new paying users and ~$20k ARR added during a 5½-month hiatus (Feb 2022 tweet); Discord "over 2,300 users" — [The New Era](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/the-new-era).
- A 2025 FinCon handout lists plans "start at $50 per year" — [lunchmoney.app FinCon 2025 PDF](https://lunchmoney.app/assets/fincon/fincon2025.pdf) (via search snippet).

### Inferences
- Lunch Money did **not abandon PWYW**. It moved the floor up roughly every 18 months, which suggests the floor is binding for a meaningful share of buyers. If most people paid well above the floor, raising it would matter little. This is an inference; the founder has not published the share at the floor.
- The 37%/60% split shows a classic PWYW distribution: a minority of high payers carries revenue while most cluster at lower prices. The headline growth coincided with the Mint shutdown, so the PWYW effect alone is not isolable.
- For Ask Linc, PWYW worked for Lunch Money as an **annual commitment with a floor and a price lock**, not as a one-off micro-payment.

### Gaps
- Lunch Money's live pricing page (403) could not be fetched, so the current maximum (was $150) and trial length are unconfirmed for October 2026.
- No published average chosen price, share at the minimum, or post-2024 metrics.
- No Indie Hackers or HN founder thread with additional numbers was located in this pass.

## 5. Trial norms and disclosed trial-to-paid conversion

### Takeaway
Trials cluster at **7 days** (Monarch, Rocket Money, Fina), **~30 days** (Copilot, Tiller, Albert, Centinel; YNAB's 34) or **no trial plus a money-back guarantee** (Simplifi as presented on quicken.com, Quicken Classic 30-day, PocketSmith 14-day). **YNAB and Doughsense explicitly need no card**, and PocketSmith and Goodbudget offer free tiers instead of trials. Kubera charges $1 for its trial. No budgeting app disclosed an actual trial-to-paid rate. Industry benchmarks put long trials at ~42.5% median trial-to-paid, and card-required (opt-out) trials convert far higher than no-card (opt-in) ones.

### Cited Findings
- YNAB: 34 days, "no credit card needed when you sign up directly through us"; students 365 days — [YNAB](https://www.ynab.com/pricing).
- Copilot: "1-month free trial" — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/copilot-track-budget-money/id1447330651).
- Monarch Core: "Your first week is on us" — [Monarch](https://www.monarch.com/pricing). Monarch Plus: 7-day trial for existing members — [PR Newswire/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/monarch-launches-premium-tier-monarch-124800073.html).
- Tiller: 30 days, card charged after trial — [Tiller](https://tiller.com/pricing/).
- Rocket Money Premium: 7-day free trial — [Help Center](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/2217739-how-much-does-rocket-money-cost).
- Albert: 30 days before being charged — [Albert Help Center](https://help.albert.com/hc/en-us/articles/21583583733271-How-much-does-Albert-cost).
- Simplifi: no trial stated; "Full payment is charged to your card immediately" — [quicken.com/simplifi](https://www.quicken.com/simplifi). Third parties report 30-day trial and/or money-back guarantee inconsistently — [CostBench](https://costbench.com/software/personal-finance/simplifi/), [Bustle](https://www.bustle.com/life/simplifi-by-quicken).
- PocketSmith: "14-day money back guarantee," no trial — [PocketSmith](https://my.pocketsmith.com/plans).
- Kubera: $1 non-refundable trial, no auto-convert — [Kubera help](https://help.kubera.com/article/117-why-pay-1-for-the-trial).
- Centinel: 30-day trial — [Centinel](https://www.centinelmoney.com/resources/centinel-vs-monarch-money). Doughsense: 14-day Plus trial, no card — [Doughsense](https://doughsense.com/compare/monarch).
- Fina: 7-day trial on paid plans (2024 data) — [toolsforhumans](https://toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/fina-money).
- **Benchmarks (cross-category, secondary):** RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 (115k+ apps, $16B revenue, mostly 2025 data): trials of 17–32 days convert at a **42.5% median** vs **25.5%** for trials of four days or less — [Subscription Insider summary](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news). RevenueCat's 2024 report had an overall trial-conversion median of 37.3% — [SaaStr summary](https://www.saastr.com/the-top-10-learnings-from-revenuecats-state-of-subscription-apps/). A benchmark roundup citing Adapty gives **49–60% for opt-out (card-required) trials vs 18–25% for opt-in (no card)** — [pricepush.app](https://pricepush.app/blog/trial-to-paid-conversion-rate-benchmarks) (secondary; not verified against Adapty's report).
- The finance-category breakout is in the gated RevenueCat PDF; public category pages found did not include finance — [RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).
- Indie data point: "The Budgeting App" newsletter reported 9,759 installs and 273 new paid subscriptions in 30 days (≈2.8% install-to-paid, computed; not trial-to-paid) — [The Budgeting App Q2 2025](https://thebudgetingapp.substack.com/p/the-quarterly-q2-2025).
- Lunch Money: trial conversion "healthier than ever" after PWYW, no figure — [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/we-lowered-our-pricing).

### Inferences
- Ask Linc's **30-day no-card trial** matches the generous end of the market (YNAB 34-day no-card; Copilot and Tiller 30-day). By the benchmarks, no-card trials convert at roughly a third to half the rate of card-required ones, so Ask Linc is trading conversion rate for top-of-funnel volume.
- The 2026 entrants (Monarch Plus, Rocket Money Premium+) use **short 7-day trials or none**. Monarch Plus's trial goes only to existing members.

### Gaps
- No budgeting or PFM company has publicly disclosed its trial-to-paid rate. The finance-specific RevenueCat figure is behind an email gate and was not retrieved.

## 6. Disclosed metrics on consumer willingness to pay (Mint migration, Monarch/Copilot growth, YNAB backlash)

### Takeaway
The Mint shutdown (announced Nov 2023, closed Jan 1, 2024) created the clearest WTP signal in the category. Monarch had its biggest signup day ever, doubled new customers, and by April 2026 reported **1 million members**. Lunch Money's MRR nearly doubled. Price increases (YNAB $84 → $99 → $109) drew vocal but anecdotal backlash, mainly over broken "locked-in" expectations, with no evidence of measurable churn.

### Cited Findings
- Mint had an estimated **3.6M active users (2021, Bloomberg)**; Intuit pointed users to Credit Karma, which "does not currently provide budgeting features the same way that Mint has" — [CNBC, Nov 7, 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/11/07/budgeting-app-mint-is-shutting-down-users-are-disappointed.html); [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2023/11/02/i-2/97189/).
- Monarch: Nov 1, 2023 "was our biggest day in terms of new users since we launched the app"; new customers roughly doubled — [TechCrunch, Nov 2, 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/02/personal-finance-monarch-intuit-mint/); [robots.net](https://robots.net/news/monarch-sees-surge-in-users-following-intuits-decision-to-close-mint/).
- Monarch raised **$75M at an $850M valuation** (2025) "to accelerate subscriber growth that took off last year when budgeting tool Mint was shut down" — [CNBC via NBC Bay Area](https://www.nbcbayarea.com/news/business/money-report/personal-finance-app-monarch-raises-75-million-despite-nuclear-winter-for-fintech-startups/3876023).
- Monarch reports "**1 million members and growing**" (Apr 21, 2026) — [PR Newswire/Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/articles/monarch-launches-premium-tier-monarch-124800073.html). At $99.99/yr list, that implies a large paid base, but Monarch has not disclosed ARR. Third-party ARR figures ($22M vs $58M) contradict each other and are unreliable — [revenuememo](https://www.revenuememo.com/p/who-owns-monarch-money) (claims ~20x paid-subscriber surge post-Mint; secondary).
- Lunch Money: MRR "nearly doubled" in about a year post-PWYW and post-Mint — [Lunch Money](https://lunchmoney.app/blog/we-lowered-our-pricing).
- **YNAB price history** (third-party): ~$45/yr → $84 (2017) → $99 (2021) → **$109 (Aug/Sept 2024)**, with the monthly price held at $14.99 — [fincomparelab](https://www.fincomparelab.com/?p=470). In a Jul 2024 r/ynab thread users flagged the $109 change — [r/ynab mirror](https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/ynab/top/?t=year). A 2021 thread: "Many people feel scammed as they thought they had lifetime pricing" — [r/ynab 2021 mirror](https://lr.us.psf.lt/r/ynab/comments/ql0h1r/deleted_by_user/hj01ls0/?context=3). Users on Lemmy cited insufficient product improvement and looked at Actual and Simplifi — [Lemmy](https://old.lemmy.dbzer0.com/c/ynab@lemmy.world). (Opinion/anecdote, not metrics.)
- Copilot: the current public price is $95/yr or $13/mo. A competitor (Mezzi) suggests legacy annual subscribers may see renewal increases, but "A headline about a price increase does not establish what every existing subscriber will pay" (unconfirmed) — [Mezzi, Sept 26, 2026](https://www.mezzi.com/blog/copilot-money-2026-pricing-change-what-annual-subscribers-should-do).
- AI-finance demand: "Over 200 million people turn to ChatGPT with personal finance related questions every month"; in a Plaid survey, 53% of consumers who used AI for finances said it helped them manage day-to-day spending — [Plaid blog, May 15, 2026](https://plaid.com/blog/chatgpt-personal-finance-plaid/).

### Inferences
- WTP for a full PFM subscription in the US clusters near **$100/yr**, and the market absorbed Mint's free users into paid apps at that price. A premium layer (forecasting, AI agent) is now being tested at **$180–$300/yr**.
- Backlash patterns (YNAB, Lunch Money's carefully worded "least annoying price increase") show users react most to **perceived broken price promises**. Grandfathering and price locks are the standard mitigation.
- The ChatGPT signal (200M monthly finance questions; Finances reaching the Free tier) means users' reference price for "ask an AI about my money" is trending toward $0. A paid offer needs to anchor on a **specific deliverable** (a forecast, a payoff plan, a shortfall alert) rather than on AI Q&A access.

### Gaps
- No primary disclosure of Monarch, Copilot or YNAB paid subscriber counts, ARR, churn, or impact from price increases.
- No data on how many Mint users went to Credit Karma versus paid apps.
- No measured churn effect for the YNAB 2021 or 2024 increases was found; evidence is anecdotal forum sentiment.
